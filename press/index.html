AboutA drawer of cold cases

You are a new detective in Violent Crimes Unit 2 at Seoul Seobu Police Station. M, a senior member of the unit, has transferred out and left you the key to a desk drawer of unsolved cases going back more than a hundred years.

There is no map to walk around. Each case comes as the documents of its period, for example a halfpenny paper in 1888 London, police files and telegram requests from 1930s Gyeongseong, a guest ledger in 1971 Seoul, a police PC in 2006 or a live group chat in 2023. Click an underlined word to write it in your notebook, then search the archives with it or ask the people involved about it. If you show someone a note that contradicts what they told you, they answer differently.

To close a case you file a report. You name the culprit and attach an evidence note to each claim, and the case is closed once all of it is correct. After the twelve main cases are closed you can read the letter M left behind.

FeaturesWhat you do

  • Read period documents: newspapers, statements, letters, telegrams, ledgers, transcripts, old personal homepages and messenger logs.
  • Keep a notebook of words and search with them. A word you have not found yet has to be typed in full, so you cannot skip ahead.
  • Question people about what you found, and press them with a note when something doesn't match. They may change what they say.
  • Use each era's tools: handwriting, footprint and voiceprint comparison, vehicle and phone lookups, close study of scene photos, cipher work, case timelines.
  • Three live cases (2021–2024) run on an investigation clock. You apply for warrants and records, some get rejected, and you have to finish before the deadline.
  • Difficulty goes from ★3 to ★5. On harder cases a rejected report gives fewer hints about what was wrong.

Red stars mark cases with graphic descriptions and crime scene photos showing bodies. A switch in the records room covers the photos and switches the text to a toned-down version with the same clues. Every case is a new story that borrows only motifs from real unsolved cases, and all people, places and organizations are fictional.
